# Output-Based Retention

## Overview

Two learners finish the same book. One writes a 300-word explainer, applies one idea, and teaches it to
a colleague. The other starts the next book. A month later only the first can still use the material —
not because of memory talent, but because output is the only step that both **tests** understanding and
**stores** it.

The design implication is blunt: put output inside the learning loop as a required phase
([[learning-acceleration-loop|Learning Acceleration Loop]]), because as a post-hoc activity it never
happens.

## What counts as output

| Output form | Tests | Cost |
|-------------|-------|------|
| Explain it to someone (or an empty room) | whether the model is complete | 5 minutes |
| Write a 1-page explainer in your own words | whether the connections are real | 30 minutes |
| Apply it to a live problem | whether it survives contact with reality | hours |
| Teach or write publicly | whether it is actually coherent | hours + exposure |

The progression is ordered by strength, and all four beat re-reading. Notice what is *not* on the list:
highlighting, copying quotes, and reorganising notes.

## The retention ordering (and its caveat)

The source material cites the widely repeated "learning pyramid": read 10%, hear/see 20%, demonstration
30%, discussion 50%, practice 75%, teach others 90%.

> ⚠️ **Use the ordering, not the numbers.** The exact percentages are repeated far more often than they
> are evidenced, and no primary measurement is cited in the source. The direction — *doing and teaching
> beat reading* — is robust; the digits are not. Do not cite 90% as a fact.

## Practical rules

1. **Output before the feeling of readiness.** Waiting to "understand it fully first" is how input loops
   run forever; the explainer is what reveals the gaps.
2. **One concept per output.** A 300-word explainer of one idea beats a summary of a whole book.
3. **Weekly share.** A recurring slot (one share per week) forces output to exist on schedule instead of
   on inspiration.
4. **Convert later.** Quarterly, turn accumulated notes into a tutorial or talk — this is the leverage
   step where learning compounds into reputation and reach, not just capability.
5. **Write to think.** Writing is not transcription of a finished thought; the sentence you cannot write
   is the concept you have not understood.

## Anti-patterns

- **Output as a reward** — "after I finish the material, I'll write about it" (never happens).
- **Output as transcription** — copying the author's structure instead of rebuilding your own.
- **Perfectionism** — polishing a public explainer before ever having explained it to one person.
- **No audience** — output with no reader degrades into note-taking; name the reader, even if it is
  yourself in six months.

## 中文速览

- **输出是检验，不是副产品**：讲不出来、写不出来、用不上，就等于没学会。
- **输出四档（由弱到强）**：讲给别人听 → 用自己的话写一页 → 用到真实问题上 → 公开教人/发表；四者都强于重读。
- ⚠️ **学习金字塔（读 10% / 教 90%）取方向不取数字**：这套百分比流传远多于被证实，源材料未给一手测量；「动手与教人优于阅读」这个方向可靠，数字不可当事实引用。
- **操作五条**：①不要等「完全懂了」再输出 ②一次只输出一个概念 ③每周固定一次分享 ④按季度把笔记转成教程（杠杆步）⑤写作就是思考——写不出来的那句，就是你没懂的概念。
- 反面模式：把输出当成学完后的奖励、照抄作者结构、公开稿打磨到完美却从没对一个人讲过。
