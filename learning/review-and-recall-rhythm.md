# Review and Recall Rhythm

## Overview

Review fails for a boring reason: it is unscheduled. A cadence converts it from an intention into a
recurring slot, and the cadence is layered so that each level does work the others cannot — the daily
keeps the material warm, the weekly finds what you never understood, the monthly finds what you avoided,
the quarterly converts knowledge into artefacts.

## The four levels

| Level | Time | What it does | Concrete form |
|-------|------|--------------|---------------|
| **Daily** | 15 min (morning) + 15 min (evening) | keeps material active, catches drift | morning: 5-min stillness + 30 min reading + review today's core goal · evening: journal + rate the quality of three decisions |
| **Weekly** | 30–60 min, ideally one meeting-free day | consolidates the week, exposes failures | one new mental-model card, one knowledge share, one failure retrospective ([[mental-models-lattice|Mental Models Lattice]]) |
| **Monthly** | 1–2 h | hunts gaps and process friction | re-derive one earlier skeleton from scratch; audit processes and adjust |
| **Quarterly** | half a day | converts knowledge into leverage | turn note clusters into a tutorial, talk, or shipped artefact |

## Retrieval, not re-reading

The single correction that makes review work: **close the source and reconstruct**. Re-reading produces
familiarity, which is the *feeling* of knowing and a poor predictor of recall. Reconstruction produces
the failure signals you actually need.

Practical forms of retrieval:

- Re-explain last week's idea from memory in three sentences, then open the note and diff.
- Answer the question the note was written to answer — without looking.
- Rebuild a small part of the skeleton ([[learning-acceleration-loop|Learning Acceleration Loop]] phase 4)
  on blank paper.
- Re-derive one claim from its base facts ([[first-principles-thinking|First Principles Thinking]]).

## The failure audit

Weekly and monthly reviews should ask, in this order:

1. What did I plan to do and not do — and why?
2. Which claims in my notes are still carrying an `[unverified]` tag?
3. Which decisions were luck, and which were process? (Probability thinking beats outcome grading.)
4. What am I avoiding?

Question 4 is the one that finds the expensive gaps: avoidance is more informative than incompetence.

## Anti-patterns

- **Review as reading** — flipping through notes and calling it review; no reconstruction happened.
- **Streak worship** — protecting a streak at the cost of doing the hard form of review.
- **Review without output** — a review that produces no diff, no corrected note, no next action.
- **Cadence inflation** — adding levels (daily → hourly) until the schedule collapses. Four levels are
  enough; consistency outranks intensity.

## 中文速览

- **复习要排进日程，否则永远不会发生**。四层节奏：日（早 15 + 晚 15 分钟）→ 周（30-60 分钟：1 张心智模型卡 + 1 次分享 + 1 次失败复盘）→ 月（1-2 小时：从零重推一个旧骨架、查流程摩擦）→ 季（半天：把笔记变成教程/作品）。
- **检索，不是重读**：合上资料再重建（口述三句、不看答案答题、白纸重画骨架）；重读只产生「熟悉感」，那是会骗人的。
- **四项复盘提问**：①计划没做的原因 ②笔记里还挂着「未核实」的条目 ③哪些决策是运气、哪些是流程 ④我在回避什么——**第 4 问最值钱**。
- 反面模式：把翻笔记当复习、为保连续打卡而回避硬复习、复习不产出任何修改/行动、节奏层级加太多导致崩溃。
