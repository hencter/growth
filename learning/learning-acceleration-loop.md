# Learning Acceleration Loop

## Overview

Every source reviewed for this note converged on the same shape: learning speed is set by **loop
closure**, not by input volume. A learner who reads ten books and outputs nothing retains less than
one who reads one book, rebuilds it from first principles, does something with it, and explains it to
someone. The loop below is the merged, executable form of the methods in the source material.

The loop has seven phases. Skipping any phase is what makes study feel slow: an unasked question
makes reading aimless, an unverified skeleton makes notes wrong, and no output makes everything
evaporate.

## The Loop

| # | Phase | Output of the phase | Failure signal if skipped |
|---|-------|---------------------|---------------------------|
| 1 | **Question** | One open, personally-owned question | Reading with no target; "study X" instead of "answer Y" |
| 2 | **Investigate** | First-hand facts, not summaries | Judgements built on someone else's paraphrase |
| 3 | **Decompose** | The base facts the question actually rests on | Solving a surface problem while the real constraint stays hidden |
| 4 | **Skeleton** | A 1-page structure from 3–5 cross-checked sources | Notes that are copies, not a model |
| 5 | **Verify by doing** | A small artefact, experiment, or decision | Understanding that collapses on first contact with reality |
| 6 | **Output** | A written explainer, share, or teaching rep | Retention decay within days |
| 7 | **Feedback** | The next question, sharpened | The loop stops; the topic is "done" and gets forgotten |

Phases 1–4 are *acquisition*; 5–7 are what turn acquisition into capability.

## Phase notes

**1. Question.** Convert closed questions into open ones before starting. "Is method X right?" becomes
"Why does X work this way, and where does it break?" A question you cannot phrase is a topic, not a
learning goal — and topics do not terminate.

**2. Investigate.** Go to the primary source before the commentary (see
[[investigation-before-judgement]]). Summaries are useful for orientation and dangerous for
conclusions.

**3. Decompose.** Use [[first-principles-thinking]]: list assumptions, reduce to what is demonstrably
true, then rebuild. This is the phase that produces transferable understanding instead of topic trivia.

**4. Skeleton.** Build the structure from **3–5 sources** at once rather than finishing one book
first. Compare where they disagree — disagreement marks the live edge of the field and is the highest-value
place to spend attention (see [[naval-learning-method]]).

**5. Verify by doing.** The smallest test that could prove you wrong beats any amount of further
reading. This is the practice half of the loop and is what makes the next phase possible.

**6. Output.** Write the explainer, teach it, or ship it. Output is a *test*, not a by-product: if you
cannot explain it, you have not learned it (see [[output-based-retention]]).

**7. Feedback.** Every output exposes a gap. That gap is the next loop's question — which is why this
is a loop and not a pipeline.

## Rhythm and scale

| Cadence | Action |
|---------|--------|
| Daily | 1–2 hours of reading inside an active loop; 15 minutes of review ([[review-and-recall-rhythm]]) |
| Weekly | 1 new mental model card; 1 knowledge share ([[mental-models-lattice]]) |
| Monthly / quarterly | Re-derive one earlier skeleton from scratch; convert notes into a tutorial |

## Anti-patterns

- **Collector mode** — accumulating sources and summaries with no output phase. The vault grows, the
  capability does not.
- **Linear completionism** — finishing every book before comparing. Reading one book cover-to-cover is
  the slowest way to build a skeleton.
- **Sequential phase collapse** — jumping from question to output, skipping investigate/decompose. Fast,
  and reliably wrong.
- **Treating review as learning** — re-reading is input repetition, not retrieval practice
  ([[review-and-recall-rhythm]]).

## 中文速览

- **学习快慢由「回路闭环」决定，不由输入量决定**：提问 → 查一手 → 拆到第一性原理 → 3-5 源搭骨架 → 动手验证 → 输出/教人 → 把缺口变成下一个问题。
- **七步缺一步就变慢**：不问问题=读得没目标；不拆解=只会背；不输出=几天就忘。
- **3-5 本书并行读**，把「各家说法不一致处」当作最该花注意力的地方。
- **输出是检验，不是副产品**：讲不出来就是没学会。
- 反面模式：只收藏不产出、必须读完一本再读下一本、跳过验证直接下结论。
